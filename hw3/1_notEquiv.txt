Prove that the statements:

    p ∧ q → r

and

    (p → r) ∧ (q → r)

are NOT logically equivalent.

Afterward, explain why you have shown that the two statements are not equivalent.
What would you expect to happen differently if both statements were equivalent?

p ^ q -> r  and (p -> r) ^ (q -> r) are not equivalent because in a situation where
p=F, q=T, and R=F, the ending values are mismatched. 

F ^ q -> r becomes F ^ T -> F becomes F -> F which ends as T
because the if was never fulfilled, so the promise never began meaning it can't be broken

(p -> r) ^ (q -> r) becomes (F -> F) ^ (T -> F) becomes (T) ^ (F) which ends as F
because one promise begins and breaks, meaning one side of the and is false, meaning it all is false

If they both were equivalent statements, p=F, q=T, and R=F should either have matching endings for both statements.
